      Sustainability

      Every empty mile is wasted fuel.

      Why empty lorries are a climate problem in the UK – and how CargoMatch helps fill them.

      The problem
      30%

      of all lorry kilometres in the UK were driven completely empty in 2024.

      That is 5,843 million km out of 19.4 billion km in total. The share was also 30% in 2023: this is a structural problem, not a temporary one.

      Source: DfT, Domestic road freight statistics 2024 [1]

      Why it matters for the climate

      Transport is the UK's largest source of greenhouse gas emissions – and freight vehicles are a big part of it.

      30% of UK domestic greenhouse gas emissions come from transport (110.4 Mt CO₂e in 2024), the largest sector since 2014. [2]
      18.4 Mt CO₂e from lorries (HGVs) – 17% of all transport emissions. [2]
      18.6 Mt CO₂e from vans – another 17%. Van emissions have risen by around 65% since 1990. [2]
      1% → 16% Lorries are only about 1% of registered vehicles but cause 16% of transport emissions (2023). [3]

      Change in emissions since 1990

      Transport is falling far behind the rest of the UK economy.

      Source: DfT, Greenhouse gas emissions from transport in 2024 [2]

      Empty lorries still burn fuel

      A lorry driving with 0% load still causes most of the emissions of a loaded trip – while transporting nothing.

      Rigid lorry 3.5–7.5 t, empty 0.74 kg CO₂e per mile
      Articulated lorry (refrigerated), empty 1.17 kg CO₂e per mile
      Example

      A single 200-mile empty return trip in an articulated lorry produces about 234 kg CO₂e – for zero cargo.

      Our calculation: 200 miles × 1.17 kg CO₂e/mile.

      Source: DESNZ greenhouse gas conversion factors 2025, diesel HGVs at 0% load [4][5]

      How we estimate “CO₂e saved” on each ride

      Every ride on CargoMatch shows an estimated CO₂e saving. It assumes that each shipment travelling on an existing ride replaces one separate trip of the same distance that would otherwise be needed. We use the lowest official factor above – a small 3.5–7.5 t lorry, 0.74 kg CO₂e per mile – so the figure is deliberately conservative.

      Example: a 290-mile ride → 290 × 0.74 ≈ 215 kg CO₂e per shipment. This is an estimate, not a measured value.

      How CargoMatch helps

      Fill return journeys

      Carriers publish trips that would otherwise run empty, so the fuel is already being burnt for a reason.

      Replace extra trips

      Goods that ride along on an existing trip do not need their own vehicle – that is where the real saving comes from.

      Built for lorries

      From 7.5 t trucks to 44 t articulated lorries – HGVs are only about 1% of vehicles but cause 16–17% of transport emissions.

      Being honest about the limits

      • Not every empty trip can be avoided. Specialised vehicles such as tankers or refrigerated lorries can only carry certain goods.
      • Freight flows are unbalanced. Some regions send out far more goods than they receive, so return trips are harder to fill.
      • The saving is the trip that no longer happens. Filling an empty lorry only cuts emissions because the goods would otherwise travel in a separate vehicle.
